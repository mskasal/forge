# instrument

Compact, honest little instruments: flat slabs, a tight grid, everything labelled.
Inspired by Teenage Engineering (EP-133 K.O. II, OB-4, Pocket Operators).

**Use for:** handheld tools, controllers, gadgets with buttons, ports or a small screen.
**Not for:** calm home objects (→ `quiet`), plain holders and boxes (→ `plain`).

## Principles

1. As little design as possible; remove features before adding them.
2. Show the engineering: ports, battery bay, screws stay visible and labelled.
3. Everything sits on one grid (2.5 mm).
4. Constraints are the look: the printer's limits shape the part, not the other way round.
5. A little play is allowed — but it comes from the layout, not from decoration.

## Form

- Flat slab or single block; flat faces; no organic or curved surfaces.
- Nearly sharp vertical corners (R0.8), small top chamfer (0.4), bed chamfer 0.4.
- Split the top into **inset panels** (0.6 mm recess) where the original uses two-tone plates.

## Interface

- Speaker / vent grilles: round holes Ø2.0 on a square 3.5 mm grid.
- Every port gets a labelled collar or an engraved frame; nothing is anonymous.
- Group related controls with an engraved line or bracket (≥ 1 mm wide).

## Graphics

- IBM Plex Mono SemiBold, UPPERCASE, engraved 0.6 mm.
- Font size ≥ 8 mm (stroke ≥ 1 mm on the A1); fewer, larger labels than the inspiration.

## Colour, material, finish

- One colour per print: Bambu PLA Matte Black. Contrast comes from geometry: insets, grilles,
  engraved lines.
- Optional accent: PLA Matte Red, as a **separate part** for the single most important control
  (power, record). Never more than one accent element.

## Do / don't

- Do: grid-aligned holes, labelled ports, visible structure.
- Don't: decorative curves, fake details, more than one accent, tiny text.
- Visible screws: part of the language, but **untested** — wait for a screw test print.
